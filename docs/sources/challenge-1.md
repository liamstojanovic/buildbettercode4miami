1. From sign-up to quarterly report
Laser Focus Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.


Marisol, the program manager: I run Laser Focus's programs, from mentoring to the workshops we give in schools. Every week I copy new sign-ups from our online form into the program spreadsheet and type in attendance from the paper sheets. I track which kid has which mentor, and I add up our volunteers' hours. None of those lists talk to each other. The same mother can be on three of them, spelled three ways, and reminders go out only when I remember to send them. Last month a kid missed three sessions before anyone called home, because the attendance sheets weren't typed in yet. Every quarter, the outcome report for our funders and the board takes me about 3 days of pulling numbers from every list, and they never match the first time. Most weeks this eats 5 to 15 hours of my time, hours I don't spend with kids and their families.


I just want to type each thing once, know which kids need a call this week, and have the quarterly report ready without losing days to it.


Once it's solved, no kid goes weeks without a follow-up, the numbers agree across every list, and the quarterly report is done the day the quarter ends.


* How far you go is up to your team: at minimum, one made-up quarter end to end: one record of sign-ups, attendance, matches and hours, the follow-ups sent, and the report built from it; as a stretch, every Laser Focus tool, an agent that drafts follow-ups and tracks replies, and edge cases like a kid who changes schools.
