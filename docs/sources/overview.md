DevDay Exchange Community Hackathon: the nonprofit challenges
Laser Focus Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.
The challenges
* 1. From sign-up to quarterly report, Marisol, the program manager: "I just want to type each thing once, know which kids need a call this week, and have the quarterly report ready without losing days to it."
* 2. Duplicate donor records, and donors who quietly stop giving, Denise, the fundraising coordinator: "I just want to know each donor is on our list once, and to hear about a late gift while there's still time to call."
* 3. From request to approved workshop, Yesenia, the front-desk coordinator: "I just want every request to turn into a confirmed workshop, with slides the school has approved, without me chasing anyone."
* 4. Private counseling notes typed twice, Reginald, a counselor: "I just want to write each note once, and know the funder sees only what it needs."
* 5. Volunteer updates lost in group chats, Owen, the volunteer coordinator: "I just want every hour counted once, and every urgent need in front of the right person the same day."
* 6. Grant applications and reports, each with its own rules, Claudette, the director: "I just want every application to sound like us and say only what's true, and every grant spent the way its funder requires."
* Bonus challenge: Cat colony updates scattered across chats and photos, Beatriz, founder of Mangrove Mews, a made-up Miami cat rescue: "I just want anyone on my team to know, the same day, how each colony is doing and what it needs."
* Open track: your own nonprofit problem: Solve a real problem that a nonprofit has, one you bring yourself.


Every project's GitHub repository must be public, with an MIT or Apache 2.0 license, by the submission deadline.
About Laser Focus Family Alliance
* Mission: to help Miami-Dade kids and their families stay safe, in school and supported.
* Serves: about 900 kids up to age 18 and 150 families a year in Little Havana and West Little River, in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole.
* Programs: mentoring, family counseling, safety and mental health workshops in schools and community centers, and community events.
* Team: nine staff, six full time (the director, the program manager, a front-desk coordinator, and a lead counselor supervising two counselors) and three part time (a volunteer coordinator, a bookkeeper and a fundraising coordinator). About 40 volunteers: mentors, event helpers and drivers who drop off supplies. Board: seven volunteers.
* Money: about $900,000 a year, mostly from about a dozen foundation, county and state grants, plus individual donors and a yearly gala.
* Tools: Excel, Google Sheets, a donor database (CRM), donation and ticketing tools, online forms, Mailchimp, QuickBooks, a secure counseling records system, WhatsApp, Canva and ChatGPT.
