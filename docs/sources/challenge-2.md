2. Duplicate donor records, and donors who quietly stop giving
Laser Focus Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.


Denise, the fundraising coordinator: I work part time at Laser Focus, raising the money that doesn't come from grants. Gifts reach us through the online donation page, event tickets, the gala auction and checks in the mail, and each keeps its own list. Every month I spend about 5 hours downloading those lists, importing them into our donor database and typing in the checks. One donor ends up there as "Rosa Delgado", "Rosa M. Delgado" and "R. Delgado", with two email addresses, so that donor gets our year-end letter three times, and our totals are wrong. Worse, nothing tells me when someone stops giving. A donor who gave every December didn't give last year, and nobody called. When a monthly donor's credit card expires, the gifts just stop, and I find out months later. Every gift we lose that way is money that never reaches kids and families.


I just want to know each donor is on our list once, and to hear about a late gift while there's still time to call.


Once it's solved, the totals are right, nobody gets the same letter twice, and no regular donor drifts away without a call.


* How far you go is up to your team: at minimum, the made-up donor list end to end: duplicates merged once staff approve, no history lost, and every late regular gift found in time; as a stretch, new gifts each week, an agent that drafts each late donor's note and tracks who was called, and failed monthly gifts caught within days.
