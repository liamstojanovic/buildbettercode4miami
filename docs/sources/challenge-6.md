6. Grant applications and reports, each with its own rules
Laser Focus Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.


Claudette, the director: I lead Laser Focus. Most of our money comes from about a dozen grants, and I write every application and report with our program manager and part-time bookkeeper. Each funder asks its own questions, with its own word limits: 250 words for one, 1,000 for the next. Rewriting the same program description takes me about half a day each time. Each grant splits its money into categories, and funders' years start in July, October or January, while QuickBooks only knows ours. Every three months, our bookkeeper rebuilds each grant's spending in a spreadsheet, about 2 hours a grant. One spending mistake could mean paying a funder back. An AI chat tool can give me a first draft, but it makes up facts and sounds like a machine, so checking it takes longer than writing from scratch. In deadline weeks, grant work takes most of our time, time that should go to kids and families.


I just want every application to sound like us and say only what's true, and every grant spent the way its funder requires.


Once it's solved, every funder gets answers that fit its questions and limits and hold only facts staff approved. The bookkeeper knows where each grant's money stands, in that funder's own year.


* How far you go is up to your team: at minimum, one made-up grant end to end: its application drafted from the fact sheet within each limit, and its spending checked by category in the funder's year; as a stretch, every grant at once, an agent that drafts each report when it's due, and a category about to run over.
